# Cursor AE interview presentation notes

Use this file as your speaker notes and final customization checklist before you present.

## Before you present

- Replace `Your Name`, contact details, and placeholder proof points in `generate_cursor_ae_interview_deck.py`.
- Swap in 2-3 quantified wins from your background:
  - quota attainment
  - average deal size or largest expansion
  - logos or segments you have sold into
  - evidence of building pipeline, not just closing inbound
- Keep the deck to 10-15 minutes. It is strong when delivered as a point of view, not a monologue.
- Practice one version that is concise and another that goes deeper if the panel asks follow-up questions.

## Slide-by-slide talking points

### 1. Title

- Open with energy and a point of view: Cursor is at the intersection of product love and a real business problem.
- Make it clear that your pitch is about how you would help turn that into durable revenue.

### 2. My thesis for the role

- Emphasize that the best AI sales motions combine bottom-up adoption with executive-level business cases.
- Show that you understand the job is bigger than "selling seats"; it is about standardization and expansion.

### 3. Why Cursor, why now

- Keep this grounded. Avoid generic AI hype.
- Speak to developer workflow impact, leadership pressure to increase output, and the need for secure rollout.

### 4. ICP and territory priorities

- Explain where you would spend time first and why.
- If you have sold into a specific segment before, connect your experience here.

### 5. My pipeline generation engine

- Demonstrate that you can source as well as close.
- Mention how you personalize outreach by stakeholder and use customer stories to create urgency.

### 6. Discovery -> POV -> rollout

- This is one of the most important slides.
- Interviewers should hear that you know how to define success criteria and run a mutual action plan.
- If you have a strong pilot-to-rollout story from your background, insert it here.

### 7. Land, prove value, expand

- Reinforce that expansion is not accidental.
- Describe how you create internal champions and map adjacent teams.

### 8. 30 / 60 / 90 day plan

- Keep this practical and execution-oriented.
- The goal is to show structure without pretending you already know every internal detail.

### 9. Operating cadence and scorecard

- Highlight that you inspect the business rigorously.
- This is a strong place to mention forecast discipline, pipeline hygiene, and deal risk management.

### 10. Handling objections and competitive pressure

- Use one or two examples from real sales experience if you can.
- Show calmness: you diagnose before defending.

### 11. Why me for this role

- Replace the placeholders with your best evidence.
- Good format:
  - one number that proves execution
  - one story that proves enterprise selling skill
  - one example that proves curiosity or technical credibility

### 12. Questions I'd ask in the interview

- Use these selectively.
- Asking one or two thoughtful questions is better than reading the entire list.

## Suggested personalization examples

You can replace the proof-point placeholders with lines like:

- "Exceeded annual quota by 131% while opening new logos in developer and infrastructure accounts."
- "Expanded a 20-seat pilot into a six-figure agreement by aligning engineering leadership and procurement early."
- "Built outbound plays for a new product category and created repeatable messaging for VP Engineering and CTO personas."

## Delivery tips

- Sound like an operator, not a marketer.
- Use direct language: problem, proof, rollout, expansion.
- If they interrupt, treat it as a buying signal. Engage, do not rush back to the script.
